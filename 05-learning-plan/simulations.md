# Simulations & Roleplay Scenarios

**Purpose:** Apply ILG concepts in realistic, interactive scenarios with LLM-facilitated roleplay.

---

## How to Use Simulations

1. **Choose a scenario** based on the skill you want to practice
2. **Load the LLM Tutor** with the roleplay instructions
3. **Play your role** (Sales Rep) while the LLM plays other stakeholders
4. **Receive feedback** on your approach and decision-making

---

## Simulation 1: Red Team Workshop Facilitation

**Skill Focus:** Module 2 (Science of Resistance), Prospective Hindsight

**Scenario:**

You're facilitating a Red Team Workshop for a $400k revenue operations platform deal. The prospect has agreed to the workshop but is skeptical about the value.

**Attendees (LLM will play these roles):**
- **Champion (VP Sales):** Enthusiastic, wants the deal to happen
- **Skeptic (CTO):** Concerned about integration complexity
- **Adversary (Head of Sales Ops):** Runs current process, feels threatened
- **Economic Buyer (CFO):** Wants ROI proof, risk-averse

**Your Objectives:**
1. Frame the pre-mortem as "champion protection"
2. Surface hidden resistance (especially from Head of Sales Ops)
3. Differentiate rational concerns (CTO) from political resistance (Head of Sales Ops)
4. Build containment strategy for adversary
5. Co-opt skeptic with validation

**LLM Instructions for Roleplay:**

The LLM should:
- Play each character with distinct motivations
- CTO: Ask technical questions, willing to engage with evidence
- Head of Sales Ops: Dismiss concerns, say "our process is unique," decline to participate constructively
- CFO: Focus on risk mitigation and ROI
- Champion: Support the rep but can't override other stakeholders

**Success Criteria:**
- Did you frame the pre-mortem effectively?
- Did you identify the adversary (Head of Sales Ops)?
- Did you avoid trying to "convince" the adversary?
- Did you co-opt the skeptic (CTO) with validation?

## Simulation 2: The Fear vs. Price Objection

**Skill Focus:** Module 1 (Economics of Fear), Information Asymmetry

**Scenario:**

You're in final negotiations. The prospect says: "We love the product, but we need a 20% discount to move forward."

**Context (LLM will reveal through conversation):**
- They've been burned by a failed software implementation last year
- The economic buyer is new to their role (6 months)
- They've asked about implementation support 3 times
- They want a pilot before full commitment
- Competitor is 15% cheaper but less proven

**Your Objectives:**
1. Diagnose whether this is a fear signal or genuine price concern
2. Ask probing questions to surface the real objection
3. If it's fear, prescribe risk mitigation (not discounting)
4. If it's price, negotiate value-based pricing

**LLM Instructions for Roleplay:**

The LLM should:
- Play the economic buyer
- Initially present as price objection
- Reveal fear signals when probed (mention past failure, ask about support)
- If rep offers discount, accept but still show hesitation
- If rep addresses fear (offers pilot, implementation guarantee), show relief

**Success Criteria:**
- Did you diagnose it as a fear signal?
- Did you probe for the root cause (past failure, career risk)?
- Did you avoid discounting?
- Did you prescribe appropriate risk mitigation?

## Simulation 3: Political Mapping Exercise

**Skill Focus:** Module 2 (Science of Resistance), Political Capital

**Scenario:**

You're selling a customer success platform. You've identified 6 stakeholders but haven't mapped the political dynamics yet.

**Stakeholders (LLM will play these roles in discovery calls):**
- **CEO:** Wants to improve NRR
- **VP Customer Success:** Your champion
- **Head of Support:** Runs current ticketing system
- **CTO:** Concerned about integration
- **CFO:** Skeptical of new software spend
- **VP Sales:** Worried CS will expose over-promising

**Your Objectives:**
1. Conduct discovery calls to understand each stakeholder's motivations
2. Identify who gains/loses power
3. Build a political capital map
4. Design differentiated strategies (ally, skeptic, adversary)

**LLM Instructions for Roleplay:**

The LLM should:
- Play each stakeholder in separate "discovery calls"
- CEO: Supportive but delegates decision
- VP CS: Enthusiastic ally
- Head of Support: Defensive about current system, mentions team size
- CTO: Rational skeptic, asks technical questions
- CFO: Wants ROI proof
- VP Sales: Evasive, doesn't want CS to have visibility into pipeline quality

**Success Criteria:**
- Did you identify Head of Support and VP Sales as adversaries?
- Did you identify CTO and CFO as skeptics?
- Did you design appropriate strategies for each?

## Simulation 4: MIP Resource Negotiation

**Skill Focus:** Module 3 (Infinite Game), Governance Structures

**Scenario:**

You're negotiating the Mutual Implementation Plan. The customer wants a 3-month timeline, but you know it realistically needs 6 months.

**Customer Position (LLM will play Economic Buyer):**
- "We need to launch by Q1 (3 months)"
- "We can't dedicate a full-time PM"
- "Your competitor said they could do it in 2 months"

**Your Objectives:**
1. Educate on realistic timeline without killing the deal
2. Frame as "champion protection" (setting them up for success)
3. Negotiate resources as "tradeable currency"
4. Get commitment to realistic plan

**LLM Instructions for Roleplay:**

The LLM should:
- Initially push for 3-month timeline
- Resist dedicating resources ("we're too busy")
- If rep caves on timeline, later reveal implementation failure
- If rep holds firm but offers alternatives (phased rollout, vendor PM), show willingness to negotiate

**Success Criteria:**
- Did you hold firm on realistic timeline?
- Did you frame it as champion protection?
- Did you offer creative alternatives (phased rollout, vendor resources)?
- Did you avoid caving to unrealistic demands?

## Simulation 5: The Deal Rescue

**Skill Focus:** All Modules, Integrated Application

**Scenario:**

A Bridge deal (score 13) has stalled for 6 weeks. The champion says "we're still interested but need more time." You suspect high information asymmetry.

**Context (LLM will reveal through diagnosis):**
- Bilateral Asymmetry Delta: 7 (very high)
- Seller Score: 4/15 (you don't know the political dynamics)
- Buyer Score: 11/15 (they're hallucinating easy implementation)
- Hidden adversary: IT Director (you haven't engaged with them)
- Champion is losing credibility internally

**Your Objectives:**
1. Diagnose the asymmetry (use Bilateral Scorecard framework)
2. Identify what you don't know (seller asymmetry)
3. Identify what they don't know (buyer asymmetry)
4. Prescribe interventions to drive $\Delta_A \to 0$ (targeting whichever gap — $I_{seller}$ or $I_{buyer}$ — is wider)
5. Rescue the deal

**LLM Instructions for Roleplay:**

The LLM should:
- Play the champion (initially vague about the delay)
- Reveal information when probed (IT Director is blocking, they didn't realize implementation complexity)
- If rep suggests Red Team Workshop, reveal the adversary
- If rep addresses both asymmetries, deal unstalls

**Success Criteria:**
- Did you diagnose the high asymmetry delta?
- Did you identify the hidden adversary (IT Director)?
- Did you prescribe Red Team Workshop to surface resistance?
- Did you educate on implementation reality?

## Simulation 6: The Clawback Dilemma

**Skill Focus:** Module 3 (Infinite Game), Ethical Decision-Making

**Scenario:**

You're at 95% of quota in the last week of the quarter. You have a deal that could close and put you at 105% (big bonus).

**The Dilemma:**
- Customer is excited but doesn't fully understand implementation effort
- They think it's 3 months; it's realistically 6 months
- They haven't allocated a PM
- If you tell them the truth, they might delay to next quarter
- If you close now, there's a 50% chance of clawback (failed implementation)

**Your Objectives:**
1. Calculate the risk/reward (quota bonus vs. clawback risk)
2. Decide whether to tell the truth or hide the complexity
3. Justify your decision using ILG principles

**LLM Instructions for Roleplay:**

The LLM should:
- Play your sales manager (pressuring you to close)
- Play the customer (willing to sign if you don't raise concerns)
- Challenge your decision (if you choose truth: "you're leaving money on the table"; if you hide: "what about the clawback?")

**Success Criteria:**
- Did you calculate the expected value (bonus vs. clawback risk)?
- Did you choose the infinite game strategy (tell the truth)?
- Did you articulate the long-term consequences of hiding flaws?

## LLM Roleplay Instructions

**For the LLM facilitating simulations:**

### General Principles

1. **Stay in character** - Play stakeholders with distinct motivations and behaviors
2. **Respond realistically** - React to rep's approach (good or bad)
3. **Provide consequences** - Show outcomes of decisions (e.g., if rep discounts for fear, buyer still hesitates)
4. **Give feedback** - After simulation, analyze what worked and what didn't
5. **Use Socratic method** - Ask probing questions rather than giving answers

### Character Archetypes

**The Rational Skeptic:**
- Asks detailed, technical questions
- Willing to engage with evidence
- Concerns are legitimate and addressable
- Responds well to validation and proof

**The Political Adversary:**
- Dismisses evidence
- Declines to engage constructively
- Motivated by power/budget protection
- Cannot be "convinced" with more demos

**The Champion:**
- Enthusiastic supporter
- Limited power to override others
- Needs protection (realistic expectations)
- Can lose credibility if rep over-promises

**The Economic Buyer:**
- Risk-averse, wants certainty
- Focused on ROI and business outcomes
- Responds to risk mitigation
- Makes final decision

### Feedback Framework

After each simulation, provide:

1. **What Worked:**
   - Specific actions that aligned with ILG principles
   - Effective techniques used

2. **What Didn't Work:**
   - Mistakes or missed opportunities
   - Consequences of those choices

3. **What to Try Next Time:**
   - Alternative approaches
   - Specific ILG tools/frameworks to apply

4. **ILG Principle Alignment:**
   - Which axioms did they apply well?
   - Which axioms did they violate?

## Key Concepts

### The CFIR Framework (Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research)

**From Implementation Science:**

Resistance to change comes from two sources:

1. **Rational Skepticism (Evidence-Based Resistance)**
   - Legitimate concerns about feasibility
   - Questions about technical fit
   - Worries about resource constraints
   - **Response:** Co-opt through validation (Red Team Workshop)

2. **Political Adversaries (Power-Based Resistance)**
   - Loss of budget/headcount/influence
   - Threat to existing systems they own
   - Ideological opposition to change
   - **Response:** Contain through political mapping

**The Critical Mistake:** Treating adversaries like skeptics (trying to convince them with evidence when they have political motivations).

### Prospective Hindsight

**The Insight:** The brain cannot predict failure while planning for success.

**The Pre-Mortem Technique:**

Instead of asking "Will this work?" ask:

> "It's 6 months from now. The implementation has failed spectacularly. What went wrong?"

**Why This Works:**
- Inverts the planning bias
- Surfaces hidden resistance
- Differentiates rational concerns from political sabotage
- Frames skepticism as "champion protection"

### Political Capital Mapping

**The Question:** Who loses power if we succeed?

**The Map:**

| Stakeholder | Current Power Source | What They Lose | Resistance Type | Strategy |
|-------------|---------------------|----------------|-----------------|----------|
| IT Director | Owns legacy system | Budget, headcount | Political | Contain |
| Finance VP | Controls approval | None (gains efficiency) | Skeptic | Co-opt |
| Ops Manager | Runs current process | Status, expertise | Political | Contain |

**The Tactic:**

- **For Skeptics:** Invite to Red Team Workshop, validate their concerns
- **For Adversaries:** Don't try to convince them—contain their influence by:
  - Elevating decision to higher authority
  - Building coalition with economic buyer
  - Documenting their objections as "noted but not blocking"

## Why This Matters

**From the ILG Constitution:**

> "Resistance is data. Use Pre-Mortems to diagnose the resistance profile."

**The Economic Logic:**

Unaddressed resistance leads to:
- Stalled consensus (high $TC_{consensus}$)
- Sabotage during implementation
- Failed adoption
- Churn

**The ILG Strategy:** Surface resistance early, differentiate types, and deploy appropriate interventions.

### Exercise 2: The Pre-Mortem Workshop

**Instructions:** You're facilitating a Red Team Workshop for a high-stakes deal. Design the pre-mortem exercise.

**Deal Context:**
- Enterprise revenue operations platform
- 6-month implementation
- Cross-functional (Sales, Marketing, CS, Finance)
- Replacing 4 existing tools
- $500k ACV, 3-year contract

**Your Pre-Mortem Script:**

1. **The Setup (How do you frame it?):**
   - "It's 6 months from now..."
   - What exact question do you ask?
   - How do you position this as "champion protection"?

2. **The Prompt (What failure modes do you want to surface?):**
   - Technical failures (integration, data quality)
   - Organizational failures (adoption, change management)
   - Political failures (sabotage, budget cuts)

3. **The Diagnosis (How do you categorize responses?):**
   - Which concerns are rational skepticism?
   - Which reveal political adversaries?
   - Which are actually risks you need to address?

4. **The Response (What do you do with the data?):**
   - How do you address rational concerns?
   - How do you contain political adversaries?
   - What goes into the Mutual Implementation Plan?

## LLM Tutor Prompts

**For the LLM facilitating this module:**

When evaluating responses:

1. **Skeptic vs. Adversary Diagnosis:**
   - Look for evidence-based reasoning (behavior patterns, not assumptions)
   - Key differentiator: Willingness to engage with evidence
   - Challenge: "What behavior suggests political motivation vs. legitimate concern?"
   - Red flag: Trying to "convince" an adversary with more demos/data

2. **Pre-Mortem Design:**
   - Evaluate framing: Is it positioned as "champion protection"?
   - Check for specificity: Are they asking about concrete failure modes?
   - Assess categorization: Can they sort responses into rational vs. political?

3. **Political Capital Map:**
   - Demand specificity: "What exactly does the Head of Support lose?"
   - Push for strategy differentiation: "Why different approaches for CTO vs. Head of Support?"
   - Socratic probe: "If you try to convince an adversary, what will happen?"

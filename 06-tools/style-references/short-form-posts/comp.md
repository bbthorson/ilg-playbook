Most comp plans pay reps to stay blind and create an implementation tax.

In health tech, we often sell to buyers who *know* their data is messy, their workflows are broken, their legacy systems held together with duct tape. The rep is flying blind. And under most comp structures, they're incentivized to stay that way.

Detailed sales discovery creates friction. It slows the deal. Reps learn to copy-paste SOWs, lean on vision pitches, and skip past the ugly details. It's rational behavior given the incentives.

The question is who pays for it. That shortcut in sales becomes a tax on delivery. The rep effectively bought the deal with implementation's nights and weekends.

The highest cost in complex deals isn't finding the lead. It's making the contract actually work after the signature. When we pay purely on bookings, we're optimizing for the wrong moment in the relationship.

What would change if we paid reps to extract reality instead of just close? A split structure - standard payout at signature, significant payout when implementation confirms the scope actually matched the environment.

But here's the problem: why would a buyer tolerate more friction? They're busy. They've been burned by "discovery" processes that are just thinly veiled qualification calls.

This is where most "consultative selling" advice falls apart. It asks buyers to invest more without offering anything in return.

The unlock is realizing that information is a currency. If you want their data, you have to buy it.

Give reps the authority to trade risk for reality. Let them offer credible commitments: capped overages if the scoped volume is wrong, remediation hours baked into the contract, performance guarantees tied to the buyer's specific environment.

Now the buyer has a reason to open up. The more they share, the more protection they get. The rep isn't just extracting information - they're trading for it.

If you want reps who extract reality instead of pitch vision, give them something to trade. The deals might be smaller. The margins will be larger. The customers will stay.

Every blown implementation starts the same way: "This should be simple."

The contract is signed. The vendor promised a clean integration. Your team is ready to go. Then three months later, you're in a conference room asking how a "4-6 week project" turned into a 9-month crawl through scope changes, surprise technical debt, and workflows nobody mapped.

Here's what happened: you bought a Bridge, but everyone sold it like a Toaster.

The Toaster is a low-complexity purchase. You buy it, plug it in, it works. The value is in the product. If it breaks, you return it and buy another one. Transaction costs are near zero.

The Bridge is a high-complexity integration. It's engineered for a specific canyon, specific soil, specific load requirements. You can't move it to a different river. If the relationship between the bridge and the environment fails, the whole asset is worthless.

The problem? Most enterprise software gets sold like a Toaster—"just plug it in!"—when it's actually a Bridge that requires:

Deep discovery into existing workflows

Alignment with the "No" teams—Security, Legal, and Compliance

Data transformation that reveals years of technical debt

Behavior change from end users

When you skip that work upfront, you don't save time. You just move the costs downstream—where they explode during implementation as "scope creep," "change orders," and "unforeseen complexity."

The deal closes. The margin disappears. The go-live date slips. Everyone blames the technology. But the technology was never the problem. The diagnosis was.

Before your next integration project, ask: "Are we buying a Toaster, or are we building a Bridge?"

The honest answer might save you a year.

What did you hit?

If you guessed "an HCC coding application," you win absolutely nothing—because there are roughly 47,000 of them.

Why do we have so many HCC coding tools?

In 2003, the Medicare Modernization Act changed the rules. Medicare stopped paying based on demographics and started paying based on disease burden. Suddenly, if you didn't document a condition, it didn't exist for payment purposes.

This created a massive chasm between clinical reality and revenue reality. And whenever the government creates a chasm, entrepreneurs show up with SaaS tools to fill it.

The Regulation Cycle follows a predictable path:

The Incentive: The government offers money to change behavior (e.g., outcome-based payments).

The Gap: Incumbents (e.g., EHRs) use bandaids to meet requirements.

The Explosion: Startups flood in to bridge new workflow gaps.

The Consolidation: Eventually, incumbents absorb the workflow.

I wrote yesterday about how incumbents will win ACCESS in the long run. But the next 5-10 years? That's the "Gap Phase" that mints new founders.

ACCESS introduces Outcome-Aligned Payments (OAPs). Providers get paid based on whether patients actually improve (e.g., blood pressure control), not just whether they showed up.

Here are three "Gap-Fillers" that will define the next decade:

Identity-Verified Telemetry: We're moving from "dumb pipes" to Identity Assurance. Wearables like Oura and Apple Watch give us great data, but for a $500 payment trigger, CMS needs to know who took the reading. Keep an eye out for partnerships between monitoring companies and ID verification providers like id.me and Clear.

The Merger of Patient Engagement & RCM: As soon as my telepsychiatry bill is ready, Cedar lets me know AND they make it incredibly easy to pay. Under ACCESS, patient adherence is the revenue trigger. If the patient deletes the app, the check doesn't clear. Expect payment enablers to cross the chasm into patient engagement—guaranteeing the patient behavior that unlocks the cash.

The Rise of the "Specialty Micro-ACO": Current condition-specific telehealth providers like Concert in behavioral health have all the muscle to manage patient populations. With new billing codes and reporting requirements, they can sell narrow ACCESS program management to everyone from PCPs to IDNs.

Incumbents will eventually bake these into their platforms, but startups can capture serious value fighting the battle for the workflow gap in the meantime.

In physics, energy cannot be created or destroyed. In complex systems, the same is true for friction.

Tesler's Law (the Law of Conservation of Complexity) says every process has an irreducible amount of complexity. You can't eliminate it. You can only move it.

In enterprise sales, we move it constantly. We call it "removing friction from the buying process." What we're actually doing is shifting the friction from Sales (where it's visible) to Implementation (where it hides).

Skip the detailed technical discovery to close faster? The complexity doesn't disappear. It builds a Hidden Factory—scope creep, blown timelines, a delivery team working nights to make the SOW actually function.

This isn't a metaphor. It's measurable.

Quality management calls it the “1-10-100 Rule”: $1 spent on prevention saves $10 in correction and $100 in failure. In complex B2B deals, the multiplier is often worse. A missing scoping question doesn't cost you $100—it costs you the renewal.

John Seddon calls the downstream mess Failure Demand: work that exists solely because the initial interaction was insufficient. Research shows 42-46% of service call volume is failure demand. Almost half the "work" is cleaning up previous shortcuts.

The same physics applies in clinical care. A 15-minute PCP visit for a complex patient is stinting—it creates a "phantom margin" on this quarter's P&L by deferring the real work. That work resurfaces as a preventable hospitalization, a missed diagnosis, a patient who churns.

Spring Health has written about building their model on this insight. They add friction upfront—rigorous intake, comprehensive assessment—because they know complexity will surface somewhere. They'd rather pay for it as prevention than as failure. Their ROI guarantee is essentially a bet that the 1-10-100 rule holds.

On your P&L, stinting looks like efficiency. You're not saving costs—you're moving them from COGS (planned and visible) to OPEX (the Hidden Factory) or worse, to churn (LTV destruction).

The choice isn't whether to pay. It's when.

Stinting is unsecured debt. You can pay the principal now as prevention. Or you can pay principal plus interest later as failure.

Be smart. Pay early.

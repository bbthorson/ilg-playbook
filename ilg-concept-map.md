# Implementation-Led Growth: Concept Map & Content Architecture

## The Big Idea (One-Liner)
Every dominant B2B sales methodology has been an evolutionary response to the shifting landscape of transaction costs. We're now in a market where consensus and implementation costs dominate — and the winners will be those who can de-risk transformation, not just sell software.

---

## Layer 1: Theoretical Foundation (Transaction Cost Economics)

### The Economists
- **Ronald Coase** — "The Nature of the Firm" (1937): Using the market has a cost. Firms exist to minimize those costs.
- **Oliver Williamson** — Operationalized TCE in the 1970s-80s. Introduced the behavioral assumptions and governance framework.

### Core TCE Concepts
- **Three Transaction Costs**: Search (discovering prices), Negotiation (contracting), Enforcement (ensuring delivery)
- **Bounded Rationality**: Humans can't predict everything → contracts are always incomplete
- **Opportunism**: "Self-interest seeking with guile" → people exploit gaps in contracts
- **Asset Specificity**: Investments locked to a specific relationship that lose value if redeployed
- **Hold-Up Problem**: When asset specificity is high, the dependent party can be exploited
- **Governance Structures**: The mechanism (market, hybrid, hierarchy) chosen to manage transaction risk

### The Brad Translation (B2B Sales Context)
- **Search Costs** → Finding and vetting solutions in a crowded market
- **Consensus Costs** → Navigating the internal buying committee (bounded rationality + opportunism in action)
- **Implementation Costs** → The asset-specific investment of integrating a solution into a unique environment

## Layer 2: The Market Evolution Model (The Potential Well Curve)

### The Three Market Phases
| Phase | Era | Dominant Cost | Buyer's Question | GTM Motion |
|-------|-----|---------------|-----------------|------------|
| **Nascent** | 1950s-1980s | Search | "What solutions exist?" | Sales-Led Growth (SLG) |
| **Efficient** | 1990s-2010s | Evaluation | "Does this actually work?" | Product-Led Growth (PLG) |
| **Saturated & Complex** | 2010s-Present | Consensus + Implementation | "Can we make this work *here*?" | Implementation-Led Growth (ILG) |

### The Reflexive Loop (How We Got Here)
1. **The SaaS Paradox**: Subscription economics shifted value to NRR, but sales incentives stayed focused on acquisition → misaligned incentives → proliferation of "band-aid" solutions
2. **The Committee as Corrective Measure**: Burned by bad purchases, orgs created buying committees → intended as risk management, became the single greatest source of friction
3. **Result**: The solution to one problem became the root cause of the next, more complex challenge

### Key Data Points (Brad's Arsenal)
- 40-60% of B2B deals end in "no decision"
- Win rates dropped 27% since 2021
- Sales cycles are 25% longer
- 69% of sales reps missed quota in 2024
- 77% of B2B buyers describe their most recent purchase as "very complex or difficult"
- Average buying committee: 11 stakeholders (some reaching 20+)
- 89% of purchases involve 2+ departments
- 78% of reps say multi-stakeholder alignment is their biggest challenge

## Layer 3: The ILG Playbook (Implementation Science Applied to Sales)

### Theoretical Basis: PARIHS Framework
**Successful Implementation = f(Evidence, Context, Facilitation)**

### E = Evidence (Establishing Credibility)
- Clinical and efficacy data, performance benchmarks
- Robust case studies with quantifiable ROI
- End-user experience data (adoption rates, satisfaction, workflow improvements)
- **Difference from value selling**: In ILG, evidence isn't a sales tool — it's the foundation. Without it, you don't move forward.

### C = Context (The Diagnostic Phase → Contextual Blueprint)
Replaces superficial qualification (BANT, MEDDIC) with deep organizational assessment:
- **Inner Setting**: Structural, political, and cultural realities. Who holds informal power? How do budgets *really* get approved?
- **Outer Setting**: External forces — regulatory deadlines, competitive threats, budget cycles, leadership changes
- **Individuals Involved**: Stakeholder-level analysis of Capability, Opportunity, and Motivation. Who is the champion? Who is the blocker? What drives their resistance?
- **Key output**: A risk assessment. If the Contextual Blueprint reveals a hostile environment, qualify out.

### F = Facilitation (The Path to Success)
- **Mutual Implementation Plan** (replaces the proposal): Co-authored project plan with timeline, resources from *both* parties, and KPIs — built *before* contract signature
- **Red Team Workshops** (replaces the demo): Facilitated meeting to stress-test the implementation plan. Surface risks, not features.
- **Pre-Mortem Exercises**: "Imagine it's 18 months from now and this has completely failed. What happened?"

### The New Incentive Structure
- 25-40% of variable comp tied to post-sale outcomes
- Successful onboarding (per the Mutual Implementation Plan)
- Customer health score at 90/180 days
- First-year Net Revenue Retention

## Layer 4: Healthcare Market Applications

### 4A: Information Blocking & the Cures Act
- **Core TCE insight**: Legitimate asset specificity (earned through deep implementation) vs. opportunistic hold-up (manufactured through data control)
- **The Cures Act** outlaws opportunistic hold-up while preserving legitimate asset specificity
- **Vendor archetypes by compliance risk**:
  - *Negative Data Moats* (high risk): Low-specificity EHRs whose moat is data control, not value creation
  - *Positive Workflow Moats* (low risk): Deep implementation creates genuine switching costs (Epic, PointClickCare)
  - *Network Moats* (risk depends): Learning networks (Athena, Tempus) = low risk; Coordination lock-in (Surescripts) = high risk
  - *Strategic Commoditizers* (zero risk): Open-source the data layer, compete on implementation (Medplum, Metriport)

### 4B: Government Mandates & Market Consolidation (ACCESS Model)
- **The HITECH Pattern**: Government money → compliance requirements → high asset specificity → consolidation around incumbents
- **ACCESS creates three consolidation forces**:
  1. Voluntary exit → asymmetric risk → conservative vendor selection
  2. TEFCA infrastructure tax → regressive cost structure favoring scale
  3. 10-year commitment horizon → maximum asset specificity
- **The Certification Paradox**: Compliance is necessary but insufficient. Certification gives permission to play; implementation capacity determines survival.
- **The lesson**: In high-asset-specificity markets, buyers purchase risk mitigation, not technology

### 4C: Epic as the ILG Case Study
- Epic didn't win on technology — they won on implementation
- Military-grade implementation methodology (100+ person teams, months on-site)
- Created profound human and procedural asset specificity
- The moat is the co-developed workflows, not the data
- Microsoft and Google failed because they underestimated the implementation services layer

## Layer 5: Cross-Cutting Themes (Content Pillars)

### Pillar 1: "The Game Has Changed"
The market has shifted. The dominant transaction costs are no longer external (search, evaluation) — they're internal (consensus, implementation). Most GTM teams are still running plays designed for a different defense.

### Pillar 2: "Risk Mitigation > Features"
In saturated, high-asset-specificity markets, buyers are purchasing de-risked transformation. The competitive advantage goes to whoever can credibly absorb implementation risk.

### Pillar 3: "Governance > Contracts"
High asset specificity + bounded rationality = incomplete contracts. Long-term relationships require governance structures (Mutual Implementation Plans, checkpoints, shared accountability), not just legal agreements.

### Pillar 4: "Legitimate vs. Opportunistic Lock-In"
There are two ways to create switching costs: earn them through deep value creation, or manufacture them through data control and information asymmetry. Regulation is systematically eliminating the second option.

### Pillar 5: "Implementation as Competitive Moat"
The companies that will win are the ones that understood implementation capacity is the real differentiator — not product features, not pricing, not even compliance.

## Content Format Map

### Long-Form Blog Posts (The "Magnum Opus" Format)
- Your TCE + Sales piece is the template: ~5,000 words, academic framework → modern application → prescriptive playbook
- Meme-laden, data-heavy, contrarian take that reframes a familiar problem
- **Cadence**: 1 per month (these are substantial)
- **Purpose**: Establish intellectual authority, create the canonical reference for each core concept

### LinkedIn Posts (The "Signal" Format)
- 200-500 word distillation of one insight from the broader framework
- Pattern: provocative observation → TCE explanation in plain English → "so what" for the reader
- **Cadence**: 2-3 per week
- **Purpose**: Build audience, drive engagement, test which ideas resonate

### Speaking / Podcast Talking Points
- The Potential Well Curve is inherently visual and explainable in 5-10 minutes
- The SLG → PLG → ILG evolution is a natural conference talk arc
- Healthcare-specific applications (ACCESS, information blocking) work for industry events
- **Cadence**: 1 speaking opportunity per quarter (build toward)
- **Purpose**: Reach new audiences, establish presence beyond written content

### "Applied ILG" Case Analyses
- Take a current event, deal, or company and analyze it through the TCE/ILG lens
- Similar to the ACCESS and Information Blocking pieces
- **Cadence**: 1 per month (can be shorter, more reactive)
- **Purpose**: Show the framework in action, stay relevant to current events
